# Business Requirements

## Status

Version 1.0 – Initial Business Requirements

Prepared during Sprint 4 discovery to define the functional and non-functional requirements for SupportOps AI. This document serves as the baseline for subsequent architecture, design, and implementation activities.

## About This Project

SupportOps AI is a fictional case study created for portfolio and educational purposes. The business scenario, requirements, and supporting documentation simulate a real-world software discovery and design process while demonstrating enterprise architecture, AI workflow design, and software engineering practices.

## Purpose

Captures the functional and non-functional requirements driving SupportOps AI,
independent of implementation detail.

## Business Problem

SupportOps Inc. receives a high volume of customer support requests across multiple communication channels, including email, live chat, and a web portal. Many of these requests involve repetitive or routine inquiries that require agents to perform the same tasks repeatedly, such as classifying tickets, verifying customer information, drafting responses, and routing issues to the appropriate department.

As ticket volumes increase, the current support process becomes increasingly difficult to scale. Human agents spend significant time on manual triage and repetitive administrative work, resulting in slower response times, inconsistent customer experiences, higher operational costs, and reduced productivity.

The organization requires an AI-powered multi-agent support platform capable of intelligently classifying, routing, and responding to customer requests while ensuring that sensitive or high-risk cases are escalated to qualified human agents. The platform must improve operational efficiency without compromising security, compliance, or customer satisfaction.

## Project Goals

The primary objective of SupportOps AI is to improve the efficiency, consistency, and scalability of customer support operations through intelligent workflow automation.

The project aims to:

- Automatically classify incoming support tickets into the appropriate support domain.
- Route tickets to the most appropriate AI specialist or human support team.
- Automatically resolve routine customer inquiries whenever it is safe and appropriate to do so.
- Escalate complex, sensitive, or low-confidence cases to human support staff.
- Reduce average response times while maintaining high response quality.
- Maintain a complete audit trail for every decision and action performed by the system.
- Provide a modular architecture that supports future expansion with additional AI agents and business workflows.

## Primary User Personas

The platform is designed to support multiple user groups with different responsibilities and levels of system access.

### Customer

Customers submit support requests through supported communication channels and receive responses generated by either AI agents or human support representatives. Customers have no direct interaction with the internal AI workflow.

### Support Agent

Support agents review escalated tickets, validate AI-generated responses, communicate with customers, and resolve issues that require human judgment or specialized expertise.

### Support Manager

Support managers oversee support operations, monitor escalations, review system performance, assign workloads, and ensure service level objectives are being achieved.

### System Administrator

System administrators manage platform configuration, authentication, user permissions, integrations, monitoring, and operational maintenance. Administrators are responsible for maintaining the reliability, security, and availability of the platform.

## Supported Support Domains

During Sprint 4, the platform will support the following customer support domains.

### Billing Support

Handles customer inquiries related to invoices, subscriptions, payments, refunds, billing disputes, and account charges.

### Technical Support

Handles software defects, service outages, troubleshooting, bug reports, application errors, connectivity issues, and technical assistance requests.

### Account Management

Handles password resets, account updates, profile changes, account verification, user permissions, and general account administration.

### General Inquiries

Handles questions regarding company information, product features, pricing, documentation, and other low-risk informational requests.

### Human Escalation

Cases that cannot be safely resolved by AI—including low-confidence classifications, high-priority incidents, security concerns, legal requests, enterprise customer issues, or repeated resolution failures—are automatically escalated to the appropriate human support team.

## Support Ticket Definition

A support ticket is the primary unit of work processed by the SupportOps AI platform. It represents a customer's request for assistance and contains all information required to classify, route, resolve, and audit the request throughout its lifecycle.

Each ticket consists of structured metadata, customer information, message content, optional attachments, and a complete history of actions performed by both AI agents and human support staff.

A support ticket remains active until the customer's issue has been resolved or otherwise closed according to the organization's support policies.

## Ticket Lifecycle

Every support ticket progresses through a defined lifecycle to ensure consistent processing, accountability, and traceability.

| **Status** | **Description** |
|----------|-------------|
| New | Ticket has been received and is awaiting processing. |
| Classified | AI has identified the support domain, priority, and confidence score. |
| Assigned | Ticket has been routed to the appropriate AI specialist or human support team. |
| In Progress | The assigned agent is actively processing the request. |
| Pending Human Approval | AI has generated a response that requires review before being sent. |
| Waiting for Customer | Additional information has been requested from the customer. |
| Resolved | The customer's issue has been successfully addressed. |
| Closed | The ticket has been completed and archived for future reference and auditing. |

The platform shall maintain a complete history of all lifecycle transitions for auditing and operational reporting.

## Ticket Inputs

Each support ticket contains both structured and unstructured information required for automated processing.

### Customer Information

- Customer ID
- Customer Name
- Email Address
- Account Tier (Free, Pro, Enterprise)
- Organization (if applicable)
- Preferred Language

### Ticket Metadata

- Ticket ID
- Submission Timestamp
- Communication Channel (Email, Live Chat, Web Portal)
- Current Status
- Assigned Agent
- Previous Interaction History

### Customer Request

- Subject
- Message Body
- Optional Attachments
    - Images
    - Screenshots
    - PDF Documents
    - Log Files

### System Metadata

- Ticket Creation Timestamp
- Last Updated Timestamp
- Processing History

## Ticket Outputs

Following processing, the platform shall generate one or more outputs that guide downstream workflows and customer communication.

### Classification

The support domain assigned to the ticket.

Examples include:

- Billing
- Technical Support
- Account Management
- General Inquiry

### Priority Level

Business priority assigned to the ticket.

- Low
- Medium
- High
- Critical

### Confidence Score

A numerical confidence score produced during ticket classification and response generation. This score is used by business policies to determine whether automated responses are permitted or whether human review is required.

### Assigned Agent

The AI specialist or human support team responsible for processing the ticket.

Examples include:

- Billing Agent
- Technical Agent
- Account Agent
- Human Support Agent

### Draft Response

An AI-generated response that may either be delivered automatically or submitted for human approval, depending on organizational policies.

### Audit Record

A complete audit log containing every significant action performed during ticket processing, including classification, routing, approvals, escalations, responses, and lifecycle state changes.

## AI Auto-Response Policy

The platform may automatically deliver AI-generated responses without human intervention only when predefined business rules have been satisfied. These rules are intended to ensure that automated responses are limited to low-risk scenarios where the platform has a high degree of confidence.

An automated response may be sent only when all of the following conditions are met:

| **Requirement** | **Condition** |
|-------------|-----------|
| Classification Confidence | Greater than or equal to 90% |
| Priority Level | Low or Medium |
| Support Domain | Billing, General Inquiry, or Account Management |
| Customer Tier | Free or Pro |
| Security Risk | None detected |
| Financial Approval Required | No |
| Human Review Required | No |

Typical examples of tickets eligible for automatic responses include:

- Password reset requests
- Invoice or billing statement requests
- Subscription information
- Business hours and contact information
- Basic account administration questions
- Frequently asked product questions

The platform shall record every automatically generated response within the audit log.

## Human Approval Policy

Human review is required whenever a support request presents elevated business risk, legal implications, financial impact, security concerns, or insufficient AI confidence.

A ticket shall require human approval if one or more of the following conditions are satisfied:

- Classification confidence is below 90%.
- Ticket priority is High or Critical.
- The request involves refunds, billing disputes, or financial adjustments.
- The request involves account security, identity verification, or suspected fraud.
- The request contains legal, regulatory, or compliance-related inquiries.
- The customer belongs to an Enterprise support tier.
- Customer sentiment indicates significant frustration, threats of legal action, or repeated escalation requests.
- Business policies explicitly require manual approval.

Human reviewers may approve, modify, reject, or replace AI-generated responses before communication is sent to the customer.

All review decisions shall be recorded within the system audit log.

## Escalation Rules

The platform shall automatically route tickets to the appropriate specialist based on ticket classification, business policies, and processing outcomes.

| **Condition** | **Escalation Target** |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Billing-related request | Billing Agent |
| Technical issue | Technical Support Agent |
| Account management request | Account Management Agent |
| General inquiry | General Support Agent |
| Security incident | Security Operations Team |
| Enterprise customer | Enterprise Support Team |
| Financial dispute or refund | Human Billing Specialist |
| Legal or compliance request | Compliance Officer |
| Low AI confidence | Human Support Agent |
| Two unsuccessful AI resolution attempts | Human Support Agent |
| Critical priority incident | Immediate Human Escalation |

Escalation decisions shall be logged and included within the ticket's audit history to ensure full traceability.

## Business Assumptions

The following assumptions define the expected operating environment for SupportOps AI during Sprint 4.

- Customer account information is available through existing enterprise systems.
- Historical support ticket data is available for context when processing requests.
- Human support agents are available during defined business operating hours.
- Customers communicate primarily in English during Sprint 4.
- AI agents may collaborate when resolving complex support requests.
- Every AI decision and system action is recorded for auditing purposes.
- Human agents retain final authority over customer communications.
- Authentication and role-based access control have been implemented before users access the platform.
- Internal business policies determine whether AI responses may be delivered automatically.
- The platform operates within the organization's existing security and compliance framework.

## Business Constraints

The implementation of SupportOps AI shall comply with the following business and operational constraints throughout Sprint 4.

### Security

- Customer data shall only be accessible to authorized users and services.
- Sensitive information shall be protected in accordance with organizational security policies.
- Role-based access control (RBAC) shall restrict access based on user responsibilities.
- All user authentication shall be performed using approved authentication mechanisms.

### Compliance

- Every system action shall be recorded in an immutable audit log.
- Customer data shall be handled in accordance with applicable privacy regulations.
- Personally Identifiable Information (PII) shall not be exposed to unauthorized users or services.

### Operational Constraints

- Human support staff shall retain final authority over customer communications.
- Business policies shall determine when AI-generated responses may be delivered automatically.
- AI agents shall not perform financial or security-sensitive actions without human approval.

### Technical Constraints

- The platform shall integrate with existing customer support systems where required.
- The solution shall support future expansion through a modular architecture.
- The platform shall be designed to support additional AI agents without significant architectural changes.

## Non-Functional Requirements

In addition to functional capabilities, the platform shall satisfy the following quality attributes.

### Performance

- Ticket classification should complete within five seconds.
- AI-generated responses should be produced within ten seconds under normal operating conditions.

### Availability

- The platform shall target an availability of at least 99.9%.
- Planned maintenance shall minimize disruption to customer support operations.

### Reliability

- Failed workflows shall support retry mechanisms.
- System failures shall not result in the loss of ticket data.
- Human takeover shall always remain available if automated processing cannot continue.

### Scalability

- The architecture shall support increasing ticket volumes without major redesign.
- Additional AI agents and support domains shall be easily incorporated into the existing workflow.

### Maintainability

- The system shall follow a modular architecture with clear separation of responsibilities.
- All major business decisions and workflow transitions shall be documented.

### Observability

- System events, workflow transitions, and errors shall be logged.
- Operational metrics shall be available for monitoring and troubleshooting.

## Out of Scope

The following capabilities are explicitly excluded from Sprint 4 and may be considered for future releases.

- Voice-based customer support.
- Social media support channels.
- Multilingual AI support.
- AI model training and fine-tuning.
- Customer sentiment analytics dashboards.
- Predictive customer support recommendations.
- Automatic financial transactions or payment processing.
- Integration with external CRM or ERP platforms beyond demonstration purposes.
- Mobile applications for customers or support agents.
- Multi-tenant deployment architecture.
